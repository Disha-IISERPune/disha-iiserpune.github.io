**Kanhewadi** Kanhewadi is a remote village about 10-15 km away from Pune-Nashik Highway. The school over there had classes from 4th to 7th standard consisting around 60-70 kids. It was a very small school having 4 classrooms with various informative chart decorations which students made by themselves. Spread the Smile volunteers had been there for 3 weekends and with the support of their principal and science teacher we had conducted various fun-based learning activities. On our first weekend we’ve started with few physics demonstrations explaining the concepts of waves and optics and their applications with the help of junk materials. They enjoyed these activities and showed curiosity of learning new things. Since we had many small kids in the school, we took an origami session in the afternoon which was the best program of the day. Almost 30 kids were present whole day with us doing various activities, playing, dancing, singing etc. That week ended by nature walk and its report writing. Second weekend we focused on chemistry demonstrations like acid base indicators, making soap, pH indicator etc. followed by periscope and solar cooker model making. We had successfully made working solar cooker in which we cooked rice. We ended with map making program in which kids made map of their village. The next week we had biology demonstrations and math puzzles followed by various competitions like poster making, drawing, origami etc. In the night we had sky watching session which was a hit, people from that village and from nearby villages came to have a look. All of them appreciated it. Since we’ve stayed there for a night every weekend we had a chat with village people and felt connected with their culture. Along with all these we learned many aspects of rural India which we weren’t aware of. The love and respect those people showed us was remarkable and unforgettable. Overall the experience of that village was astounding, entertaining and most importantly educating for all of us.

**Kurunji** Kurunji is a remote village located around 20 km from the Pune-Bhor road. This time we went to a boarding school in this village (Ashram shala). Children from different parts of Pune were enrolled in this school. Effectively, the activities were conducted for two weekends. On first weekend, we showed them some physics experiments related to light for example reflection laws using mirrors and pins, Newton’s ring, for the children below 10 years of age, we took some writing exercises and puzzle solving. The children also prepared kaleidoscope, periscope. It was an interesting session for them. On second weekend, mainly, we showed them some interesting chemistry experiments. This was just to introduce to the colourful world of chemistry. These included flame test, iodine test for starch, invisibility ink. Also, we showed them how a microscopes work. We also prepared a blood smear and showed it under the microscope. Children were amused after observing it through the microscope. That night, we took a small session on hygiene especially for girls, as these girls have to live alone away from their parents. Next day, in the morning, we all prepared some special glasses using gelatin paper and showed them some 3-D videos through those glasses. The children were amused to see the animals approaching them through the screen. It was a really nice experience to live with those children, to know their problems. Also, I was happy to see the smile on their faces, which showed that the program literally served its purpose of Spreading the Smile.

**Kondhanpur** Spread the smile as the name itself suggests is an initiative by IISER Pune Students to bring smile to faces of those school kids who are deprived of the scientific world that we live in. Continuing what we did in last 2-3 years, we went to a village named Kondhanpur. During the first session in the school, we showed them some of interesting science experiments based on the concepts of Refraction of light, Buoyancy, leaf color etc. In the second session we went to the village & we demonstrated some more experiments. Also we did Paper craft (I don’t remember the name!) in that mean time. Also we showed them some movies which they really liked. On the next day it was republic day, so not many activities, that were planned previously, were conducted on that day. During the next weekend, we got a much better response from kids as well as from school. We went & did some different experiments (I forgot most of them!). This weekend was more fun & a good experience. All in all, it’s a good time to spread some smile.

**Mangdhari Primary** This was a village 12 kms from Nasrapur, Bhor district. One can describe it as a typical village in the Western Ghat region, with greenery all around, near hills and a river nearby. The village had a primary school. We conducted different activities there for 4 weekends, starting from 25th-26th January to 15th-16th February, 2014. Activities included science sessions, where we demonstrated experiments covering topics from density, magnetism centrifugal force to evaporation. We also had model making session where we taught them how to make periscope and kaleidoscope. Crafts session included making frogs, ducks and other animals using paper. We also made paper hats which the children really loved and were walking all around the village in the evening wearing those hats. Games session included playing games like cricket, langdi catch. Movie screening sessions were held in the evenings where we showed them movies like Harishchandranchi Factory, Charlie Chaplin, Duniyadari. Star gazing session was held one weekend at night. Sunday mornings were used for field activities like trekking and map making. Even though each weekend was packed with activities, we still found time to interact with the kids and their parents informally. We got to know about their lives and they got to know about ours. Conducting those sessions were truly fun-filled. The response to the activities was amazing. Those four weekends were memorable and I’m looking forward to visit those villages again this time.

**Mangdhari Secondary** On my tour to Mangdhari village for STS Disha program I was surprised to get huge support and co-operation from the Principal and the local villagers. As I heard from many volunteers, the teachers in many villages don’t co-operate and hence I was a bit nervous while going to this village. Finally when we reached there, the principal was there already and was waiting for us. He was in full support with the idea that, for motivating students towards science, we should show the beauty of science rather than just give textbook knowledge. He was there entire time and helped us with scientific Marathi words. Many teachers took experiment videos from us to show the kids. I felt really happy as they were trying to improvise the school and provide students the best they can. Also the support from villagers and positive response from students enabled us to properly implement the program. In first week of our program we started with demonstrating science experiments. We explained concepts of wave and optics through straws and scotch tape. We took origami sessions for which 20-30 kids were there. Second week was the most fun week of all. We did map making sessions, explained concepts of refraction and reflection of light through experiments. In third week we moved towards some chemistry and biology experiments, of which, students enjoyed the Game theory experiments done there. In fourth week, experiments regarding Electricity and magnetism were done. Students also prepared Periscopes with the help of the volunteers. On the 2nd and 3rd week we showed them movies of Charlie Chaplin, Harishchandrachi factory etc. at night for entertainment. The moment we spent with children, how they bowled us out in very 1st bowl, the dinner we had in one of their houses, the smile they had when they understood the logic behind the experiment, these all were the best and touching moments of STS program. I will recommend everyone to experience this at least once.

**Male** ‘Spread the smile’, as its name aptly suggests, intends to bring smile on little faces by opening new door of fun education to them. It is an attempt, to interact with village kids; to know their problems and share our knowledge as much as we can, with them. In the process, you get to learn lot of new things and end up changing your perspective towards rural India. My first ‘spread the smile’ experience was not only exciting but thought- provoking and was indeed, memorable.

WEEK 1 We, group of 6 IISER Pune students visited a village called Male, about 50 Kms from Pune. Being surrounded by hills and greenery, the village is gifted with a mesmerizing natural beauty. The school, named ‘Senapati Bapat high school’ lies along the roadside, has a fairly big campus. We did four different demonstrations, targeted to students from 5th to 9th std. 1) simple pendulum 2)stethoscope demonstration 3) explanation of reflection and refraction through fluorescent solution using laser.4) observing tree through telescope. Each activity got enthusiastic response from children. Even teachers accompanying them were keen to know the concepts behind each activity. But it becomes sometimes really difficult task, to control children’s enthusiasm, especially when you let them handle apparatus. In a way, we enjoyed that, too. In afternoon, we demonstrated an interesting experiment called chameleon reaction, in which chemical changes its colour on shaking. The experiment was a patience test, but as reaction progressed, kids couldn’t contain their excitement. One of the girls even did it on her own. It was indeed new thing to thing to them. In meantime, some other kids were shown a documentary called ‘ Journey to the edge of universe’ .They found it quite interesting, even though didn’t understand what it was all about. Being grown up in a village, they show interest in anything and everything you show them, whether that be a demonstration, an experiment or a model. Even if they don’t understand all these things, they seem to enjoy everything. This is the thing, worth learning from them. The highlight of this whole ‘endeavour’ was sky watching session which got really good response from children as well as villagers. Everyone, including volunteers was filled with enthusiasm and excitement as we all approached the ground where session was going to held. Being in a village, we were naturally gifted with a clear and dark sky which added to success of this session. Our village coordinator, who is a passionate astronomer, was patiently explaining everyone about the planet Jupiter and other galaxy which was being shown. Thus, the day ended with tasty dinner made by one of the villager woman. Next day, we had map-making in our plan, prerequisite of which was a visit to village. But enough number of kids didn’t turn up. So, after having breakfast, we visited the river nearby and left the village. Definitely, one day wasn’t enough to interact and to give them everything we wanted to give. This made some of us arrive here again; in the next week.

WEEK 2 This time, we were 6 volunteers, 3 from IISER and 3 non-IIserites (from other colleges in Pune). We had electrolysis experiment, motor making, origami session and map-making in our plan. Electrolysis experiment and motor making demonstration were done in morning session followed by lunch and origami session in afternoon. Electrolysis experiment, intended to give concept of electrolysis was done with 7th std students. Even though the concept was not there in there syllabus, some students managed to grasp it fairly. In meantime, when reaction was taking place, we kept students busy, challenging them with simple maths puzzles. The afternoon Origami session got quite a good response. Children were eager to come even in warm afternoon after a lunch. This time, we got some time to explore the village and area around the famous dam in village. Being an evening time, it was a bliss spending time there with cool breeze filled in the clean atmosphere around. This is one of the good things, which ‘Spread the Smile’ offers. You spend one whole day in village. You get the chance (little it may be) to know a village and you actually start appreciating village life. Next day, we started map-making session after breakfast. Kids surprisingly were well informed about their village and small details such as landmarks and directions. Thus, some of the girls managed to finish it quickly and helped others with the same. After spending some time with children, we left the village, storing smiles on those little faces in our hearts, forever.
