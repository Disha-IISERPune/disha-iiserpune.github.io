Disha IISER Pune part of its ‘Spread the Smile’ programme to conduct workshops in villages, visited the village of Kusgaon, located off the town of Shivapur on the Pune-Bangalore route.

On the first weekend, 10th and 11th of March, the session began with a demonstration of toys and science experiment designs by Aravind Gupta. The demonstrations consisted of siphon, levitating pencil, motor (simple and magnificent), centrifugal pump (straw and valve tube), toothpaste pump, hovercraft, balloon bottle, straw flute, trumpet and balloon rocket. The volunteers had practised these demonstrations on the previous day and after the demonstration, the students were allowed to try them out for themselves and learn things on their own.

In the afternoon, a session on arts was conducted. A session on leaf printing was conducted and the children painted design onto paper using leaves dipped in paint. Later, a project session was conducted. The girls were split into groups according to their age and topics of project were decided. These projects were based on the problems faced by the village as a community and were supposed to extend through the four weeks ending in a solution to the problem. The topics chosen were very relevant to their village, such as water harvesting, water cycle, de-addiction, ill-effects of quarrying and waste water management. Each group consisted of 4-5 members and were mentored by the volunteers. In the first weekend, they chose their topics and decided what issues they wanted to address under the broad headline.

After these discussions, conveniently placed, was a refreshing session of sports and games. The game for girls was one commonly known as _Dukkar Musandi._ The players are divided into two teams and the turns of each team are timed. When the time begins, two players of one team enter the field from one corner with their hands locked (tied) behind their backs. They then try and head-butt players from the opponent team who enter from the diagonally opposite corner, three at a time, and can run around in the field. If a player manages to touch any opponent with their head, he is out. For the boys, the games played were different. One was _Rumal Pani_ in which same numbered players from opposite teams competed for the property of a handkerchief placed in the middle. You are out if you couldn’t get the handkerchief back or if you are tapped by the opponent while in possession of it. The second game was _Kombda Yuddh_ in which, again, same numbered players from opposing teams competed to catch the other off balance while hopping holding the other leg with one hand. The team with maximum individual points wins. Another game was _Ghodesavar Yuddh._ In this game, each team has to have even number of participants and they have to carry one on the back. When the time starts each pair has to enter the field and start pushing and shoving until the opponent loses balance. The team with the last pair standing wins. Yet another game was _jahaaz_ in which two circles have to be mad by locking hands of team members. Each team has to try and break the other circle. _Dharmaguru_ was another game played in which there is a designated _dharmaguru_ for each team who sits at a location. The members of one team have to catch hold of the members of the other team and bring them to the _dharmaguru_. They are out of the game if the opponent _dharmaguru_ touches him. The game ends if all members of one team including the _dharmaguru_ are out of the game.

The next morning, the activity planned was map-making or cartography. The children were divided into four groups and they were taken to four different directions. They were then asked to make a map of the village, a map to scale. This session lasted a full two hours. After this, the volunteers returned after lunch.

The next weekend, 17th and 18th March, began with a session on Biology experiments. The volunteered carried four microscopes from the IISER Biology laboratory and demonstrated experiments at the school. The experiments were of basic slide preparation with onion peel cells, leaf cells, flower petal cells, pollen grains, stem, _Spirogyra_ (in water) etc. They were taught how to make temporary mounts and how to focus things under a microscope. The volunteers also taught them how to dissect a flower and observe its parts and this was done using a _Hibiscus_. Another experiment on osmosis using raisins put in water was also carried out. However, this did not work properly because there wasn’t enough time for the raisins to grow to full size.

In the afternoon arts session, the volunteers taught the children a few stitches used in hand embroidery. They made handkerchiefs with mended sides and the corner decorated with flowers made of French knot and single chain stitches. After this, the girls had a sports session in which they played a few of the games described above, such as _Rumal Pani_, _Kombda Yuddh_ and _Ghodesavar Yuddh_. The boys then played the same games as played in the previous week along with a new one called ten passes. The aim of the game is to make ten consecutive passes of the ball to your team members without letting any opponent get hold of the ball and not letting the ball touch the ground. The boys seemed to enjoy these games and heartily took part in all of them. During this time, a session on waste management was conducted for the girls. Groups of girls collected the litter in different areas around the school campus and piled them. Then the volunteers told them the importance of waste management and the importance of segregating the waste and disposing off. The biodegradable and the non-biodegradable wastes were segregated and disposed suitably.

The next morning, the weekly project session was conducted in which each group laid groundwork for their project by speaking to knowledgeable people of the village and observing the surroundings. The group on waste in water management noted down places where there was dirty water in the village and their sources; the team on de-addiction spoke to the elders of the village who gave information on the ill-effects of addictions and the possible diseases caused; one team talked to the people living next to the quarry in the village to find out the harm that it has caused; the last team on water harvesting spoke to a group working on the same thing and also visited a dam.

After an exhausting morning of work, the students were shown a movie, ‘I am Kalam’. Then after a filling lunch, the volunteers returned.

The third weekend, 24th and 25th, at Kusgaon saw visits by Dr. Srivatsan of IISER Pume. He demonstrated a lot of fun Chemistry experiments such as acid-base indicators, phenolphthalein, colour changing reaction (a redox reaction using glucose, two dyes and NaOH), universal indicator, red cabbage indicator, burning alcohol, making bouncy rubbery balls using synthetic polymer made with synthetic gum and borax, chemiluminescence and elephant toothpaste.

The next session was the weekly project session, where the students made the respective modes for their projects using the stationery, which they had asked for in the previous week. Three dimensional models were made using cardboard, straws, paper, glue, plastic bottles, mud, leaves etc. This was followed by a fun sports session in which they all played kho-kho, round runs (which is very similar to baseball), Chirghodi (in which a team makes a long line bending and holding the legs of the one in front and the other team members have to leap over them and test the strength of this ‘bridge’).

The next morning, the same project session continued in which they completed the model making. The models made were well-researched and innovative. The ideas that went into the making of those models were very creative and novel. After this, they were shown a movie – ‘Stanley ka dabba’. Then everyone dispersed for lunch and the volunteers returned. The volunteers, on the way, also visited AFARM, an institute at Shivapur which has a home-based water harvesting model.

The fourth and final weekend was set aside for the exhibition of the work done by the students. The village was celebrating a festival or _jatra_ and it was decided that the models will be displayed during the festival. The morning session in the school turned out to be a fun session where the students were shown math puzzles, games and brain-teasers using the kits borrowed from the Innovative Science programme by NCL-IISER. The puzzles included making a cube using blocks, making a square using symmetric pieces, a few games using properties of magnets, making a pyramid using arrays of balls, Newton’s cradle for testing the Principle of Conservation of momentum, practical proofs of Pythagoras theorem and algebraic identities, coin moving games, Towers of Hanoi etc. There was enthusiastic participation from the children and everyone was eager to solve the puzzles and play the games.

In the afternoon, though it was the girls who made the models, since they had chores at home, the boys helped to touch up the models that they had made. The finishing touches were made by evening and an exhibition was held in the school yard which is right next to the temple where the festivities were going on. The models were displayed and a small banner was made and hung in the backdrop. Again, since the girls were busy with the guests at home, they instructed the boys in the camp on what to explain to visitors. The boys enthusiastically took up the job and explained the models and projects very well. The exhibition was visited by the _Panchayat_ members and other important members of the village community and the efforts of the students were appreciated by all. A few of the models had novel solutions to common everyday problems in the village and these did not fail to impress those who came to see it.

At sundown, the volunteers cleaned up the place and bade farewell to the children and promised to return. The children of Kusgaon are very enthusiastic and motivated and they seem to value the kind of exposure that is being given to them. Most of the children heartily make full use of the opportunities provided to them and experiment and learn a lot. They are also very creative and full of ideas. Though they might be late for a few sessions and we might have to go personally and call them from their homes, we know that it is not lack of interest or boredom that causes these delays but genuine reasons like chores at home. It was quite challenging for the volunteers to shift themselves from an urban scene to a rural scene. However, they managed to get a slight glimpse into the lives and issues of a rural environment.
